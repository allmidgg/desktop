The app

Six things it does — all of them from the client you already have

No injection, no memory reading, no DLL in the game process. It talks to the same local APIs Riot ships with the client, which is why it is safe to run and why it can never show you anything the game did not already tell everybody.

Champion select

Champion select

Your masteries, set before the timer runs out

Tick one box and the app keeps your mastery page matched to whatever you picked, including when you change your mind halfway. Underneath it: what wins on your champion in your lane, who you are up against, and which counters actually beat them.

In-game overlay

In-game overlay

Objective timers, on top of the game

A dragon falling puts a banner on all ten screens, so counting forward from it is arithmetic, not information you were not given. The panel carries that, the gold difference in items on the field, your skill order so far, and a nudge when your trinket slot is sitting empty.

Builds

Builds

What people build, not what somebody thinks they should

Items, boots and summoner spells ranked by what actually won, per lane, with the number of games behind every row. No editor's picks and no theory -- if a build is listed it is because people played it and it worked.

Matchups

Matchups

Counters that hold up per lane

A matchup pooled across every lane tells you a bot-lane marksman beats a mid-lane mage, which is true and useless. Ours are computed per lane and filtered by how often that pairing actually happens, so a counter is a counter where you will meet it.

Every number

Every number

Sample size attached, always

A 62% win rate over nine games is noise wearing a percentage. Every figure on the site and in the app carries the count it came from, and anything too thin to mean something says so instead of showing a number.

Local first

Local first

No account, no telemetry, open source

It reads your own client on your own machine and keeps its database in a file you can open. Sharing your games is a switch you turn on, not a condition of using it. The whole thing is MIT-licensed and the source is public.

Champions

All 173, and we tell you which ones we have numbers for

63 champions carry win rates, builds and lane matchups from 125,096 recorded games. The rest have a page too — with Riot’s published base stats and a straight answer about why there is no percentage on it yet.

Where the data comes from

Nobody covers League Classic. So we counted it ourselves.

Riot’s public API does not carry Classic — its map and its queues are absent from their own published lists, and its match history is deliberately not exposed. So the numbers had to come from somewhere else: the client’s own local APIs, and players who chose to share the games they played.

125,096 games across 362,263 players, all 63 champions, split by lane, with every sample size attached.

The overlay

What it puts on your screen, and what it never will

On the panel

  • Objective respawn timers, counted from a kill the whole lobby watched
  • The gold difference in items on the field
  • Your own skill order, as you level it
  • A nudge when your trinket slot is sitting empty

Never on it

  • Enemy ability cooldowns
  • Ultimate timers on portraits
  • Ward positions
  • Anything the game did not already show both teams

Not because we could not build it — because Riot’s third-party rules forbid exactly this, and a tool that gets you banned is not a tool.
