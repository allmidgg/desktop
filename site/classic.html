What the data says

Three things 125,096 Classic games make obvious.

Each of these comes straight out of the match data below, with the sample size it rests on.

Finding 01

Tryndamere is the strongest champion in Classic

He wins 58.1% of 23,124 games. The weakest, Lux, sits at 42.5% over 18,274 games — a spread of 15.6 percentage points from top to bottom of the roster.

Finding 02

47 of 63 champions are played in all five lanes

Not a handful of games — enough to clear the 100-game bar in every single lane. Classic has no role queue, so support Tryndamere and jungle Annie are not memes here, they are sample sizes. That is why a modern tier list tells you nothing about this mode.

Finding 03

The most lopsided matchup is Heimerdinger into Garen

Heimerdinger takes 68.6% of 155 head-to-head games. Of every matchup with at least 100 direct meetings, none sits further from even.

What you get

Everything the modern overlays give you, for the mode they ignore.

Champion select scouting, masteries that set themselves, and builds behind every entry in that tier list.

Know the lobby before it locks

The moment champion select opens, AllMid reads it and tells you what you are walking into.

  • Who picked what, on both teams, as it happens
  • Likely role for every player, based on what they actually play
  • Counters ranked by real matchup win rate, not gut feeling
  • Team bans, so you can see what got taken away

Masteries set themselves

Tick the box once. From then on, every time you change champion in select, your mastery page is rewritten to the best 30-point setup for that champion and role — well before the timer runs out.

  • Always a legal page: 30 points, every prerequisite respected
  • Re-applies on every champion change, not just the first one
  • Your existing pages are backed up before anything is touched

Builds behind every number

Open a champion in the app and you get the build the wins were actually played with.

  • Most-built items with how often they were held and how often those games were won
  • Summoner spells, ranked by how often the pair was taken
  • Matchups — who beats it, who loses to it, and by how much
  • Win rates smoothed, so a 3-game 100% never tops the list

Where the numbers come from

There is no API for Classic. There is a way around it.

Riot’s public API returns nothing usable here: every Classic match ID comes back forbidden and every Classic queue filter comes back empty. But your own game client knows everything about the games it can see.

STEP 1

Your client already knows

League runs two local APIs on your machine for its own interface to use. AllMid reads them — the same match history you can open yourself, just faster.

STEP 2

It walks the graph

Every match names ten players, and every player has a match history. Follow that outward and a handful of games becomes a hundred thousand.

STEP 3

Finds get pooled

Games are merged and deduplicated by ID. If ten people run into the same match it is stored once, so overlap between users costs nothing.

STEP 4

Everyone gets it back

The pooled numbers are what this page is built from. Every new person who runs the app makes the tier lists, counters and builds here sharper — the app itself still computes from what it has crawled locally.

Per player: the account identifier (puuid), champion, team, position, result, KDA, CS, gold, final items and summoner spells. Per game: id, timestamp, duration, queue and patch. No display names, no runes or masteries, and no purchase order — the match history does not carry one. The puuid is what makes deduplication and player lookup possible; it is the same identifier the client itself uses, and it is listed here because leaving it out would understate what leaves your machine.

Before you run it

You are about to run an .exe next to your game. Here is exactly what it does.

Overlays have a bad reputation, and often a deserved one. The honest answer to “is this safe” is not a promise — it is a description you can go and check, which is the whole reason the source is public.

Does not

Read or write game memory. No injection, no DLLs, no hooking into the game process.

Does not

Touch your credentials. No login, no account, no password field anywhere in the app.

Does not

Modify game files. Nothing in your League folder is written to or replaced.

Does not

Play for you. No in-game automation, no scripting, no input simulation.

Does

Use the client’s own local APIs — the interfaces League already runs on your machine for its own UI.

Does

Write your mastery page, and only when you ask it to. Your existing pages are backed up first.

Does

Send collected match data to the shared pool, so the stats improve for everyone. There is a switch in the title bar that turns it off, and it shows you what has been shared.

Does

Build in the open. The installer comes out of a public CI run you can inspect, not off somebody’s laptop.

Rather verify than trust? Clone the repository, then run npm install and npm run build. You get the same application, built from the source you just read.
